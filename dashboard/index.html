Capacity Planning Forecaster

Emergency medical calls for New York City, by dispatch area and borough, rolled up to the city. Forecasts with uncertainty ranges that are measured rather than assumed, reconciled so the parts sum to the whole, and turned into a staffing number at a service level you choose.

Did the intervals hold?

A 90 percent interval should contain the outcome 90 percent of the time. This is the share it actually contained, in a trailing window, for three ways of building the interval around the same seasonal naive forecast. Width is underneath, because an interval reaches any coverage you like by being wide enough, and coverage bought with width is not a result.

What conformal coverage promises. Split, adaptive and aggregated conformal all guarantee coverage on average over time, and only if the forecast errors are exchangeable. Demand through a shift is not exchangeable, so no coverage is promised in any one window, and the chart above is what that looks like when the assumption fails: adaptive conformal cannot widen past the largest error in its calibration window, so it does not recover inside the shift however fast it adapts.

Do the parts sum to the whole?

Change in CRPS against the unreconciled forecast, and coverage at the nominal level. Negative is better: CRPS is a loss. Every number is a point estimate with a 95 percent moving-block bootstrap interval over forecast origins.

The rota, priced

Units staffed a day and what that cost, summed over the dispatch areas and averaged over the days ahead, against an oracle that knew each day's demand and so cost nothing. Every number carries its 95 percent interval.

What this page does not show

  • Coverage is measured on seasonal naive wrapped in each conformal method, at one level of the hierarchy, so that the three methods are compared on one forecast rather than three.
  • The service level moves the staffing table exactly, because the table was computed at each of its stops. The line it draws on the forecast is interpolated between the bands that were exported, and is there to be read, not measured.
  • The decision inputs are illustrative. They are a table in the repository, not constants in the code, and every number below them moves when they are replaced.
  • Nothing here is live. Every number comes from one rolling-origin backtest over published data, and the repository's README carries the same numbers as tables, with the models this page does not draw.
